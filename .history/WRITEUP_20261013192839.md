# Write-up

**Task 0 — Description.** The revenue file contains one row per company and one column per quarter, which
isn't tidy. Quarter should be a single column, not 12. `orders` and `shipments` are both already tidy
on their own.

**Task 1 — Reshape.** `melt(id_vars="company", var_name="quarter", value_name="revenue")` turns the 12
quarter columns into one `quarter` column, which gives us 60 rows that each contain one company-quarter pair
(see output).

**Task 2 — Duplicate keys.** `order_id` is unique in `orders` but repeats in `shipments`, since some
orders ship in multiple parts. This means joining the two is one-to-many.

**Task 3 — Join and compare means.** Joining `orders` to `shipments` duplicates rows for orders that got shipped 
in more package/shipment. This skews the average order amount per customer (e.g. C107: 106.0 joined vs. 96.5 correct).
**The correct mean comes from `orders` alone**, since `amount` belongs to the order and not the shipment.

**Task 4 — Analysis-ready table(s).** Revenue and orders/shipments don't share a common key, so combining
them would mean we are basically coming up with a relationship that doesn't exist. I kept them as two separate 
tidy tables: `revenue_analysis_ready.csv` and `orders_analysis_ready.csv`.

**Task 5 — Visualization, before vs. after.** Before tidying, I ransposed the table first to plot revenue.
After, it's one line — `sns.lineplot(data=revenue_final, x="quarter", y="revenue", hue="company")` 
where the legend is built in automatically.
